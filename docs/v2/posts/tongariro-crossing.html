Featured on National Geographic's list of most epic day hikes, the Tongariro Alpine Crossing is undoubtedly a must-do in New Zealand. Get ready to traverse otherworldly volcanic landscapes that served as the backdrop for Mordor in the Lord of the Rings films.

Doing the trek requires a booking with DOC, a relatively recent introduction in 2023 to better monitor visitor numbers. At the time of my booking in January 2025, there were no visitor caps and booking was free. However, I believe this may be changing in the near future, so please do check the requirements on the official DOC site before your visit.

Lodging and Transportation

The nearby towns of Whakapapa, National Park and Tūrangi are all good options to stay at, with shuttle services to the trailhead readily available. If you are set on doing the hike, it is advisable to allocate a few days extra in case of bad weather.

I would recommend staying in Whakapapa as it is closest to the many popular trails in the area, such as Taranaki Falls and Tawhai Falls (Gollum's Pool). I stayed at an airbnb in National Park, and besides the limited food options (expected), I had no complaints.

For transport, I booked a shuttle with Tongariro Crossing Shuttles, which costed $61.14 NZD. I chose a pickup time of 7:15 AM at a hostel not far from where I was staying. Be familiar with your fitness level, and allocate enough time to comfortably catch your shuttle back at the end of the hike.

Preparation

As with any hike in New Zealand, weather conditions will heavily influence the difficulty and safety of your journey. Be sure to check the weather conditions on the day and pack accordingly. Even up till the day before the hike, I was worried that the shuttles would be cancelled as the forecast wasn't kind that week.

Thankfully, we got the confirmation that the hike was on, even though the forecast was a little iffy.

The Hike

Distance: 20.2km one-way
Elevation Gained: ~800m
Duration: ~6-8 hours

After being dropped off bright and early at the Mangatepōpō carpark and a quick toilet break, you'll be off on your own merry way. Set off at a comfortable pace as the first hour through the Mangatepōpō Valley is a nice flat stretch, with plenty of boardwalk mixed in.

Soda Springs

The sun was absolutely brutal that day and by the time I reached Soda Springs about an hour in, I had already shed all my extra layers.

The next stretch is called the Devil's Staircase, a series of stairs that climb up and up and felt like they would never end. Enjoy temporary relief as you pass through South Crater, and finally, up Red Crater, where you can marvel at views of the surrounding volcanic peaks. From there, you descend down some seriously steep volcanic scree to reach the famous Emerald Lakes, a popular spot to take a break for lunch. The final descent into Ketetahi is through bush, which after four hours of exposed volcanic terrain feels almost absurdly pleasant. Bellbirds calling. Actual shade.

South Crater

Up for a challenge? The Tongariro Alpine Crossing offers a completely different experience when done in winter, and going with a guide is highly recommended. The Tongariro Northern Circuit is a multi-day trek that offers a more immersive experience of the Tongariro National Park.
