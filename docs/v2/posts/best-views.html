A completely subjective list of the best views I found in New Zealand. 'Twas a tough fight!

1. Nugget Point, The Catlins

Nugget Point is a rocky headland on the southeastern coast of the South Island, in the Catlins region. The view from the lighthouse is stunning, with the rugged coastline and the "nuggets" (rock formations) jutting out of the ocean. The Catlins is also a great place to spot wildlife, including seals, penguins, and seabirds, making it a must-visit for nature lovers.

Out of all the lighthouse viewpoints I've visited, this one takes the cake.

2. Mount Sunday, Ashburton

Mount Sunday is a solitary hill located in the Ashburton District of the South Island. It is famous for being the filming location of Edoras, the capital city of Rohan, in the Lord of the Rings film trilogy.

The view from the top of Mount Sunday is breathtaking, offering panoramic vistas of the surrounding Canterbury Plains and the Southern Alps. The hike to the summit is relatively short and accessible, making it a popular destination for tourists and fans of the films alike.

3. Cable Bay, Nelson

Cable Bay is a picturesque coastal area located near Nelson in the South Island. The bay is known for its stunning views of the Tasman Bay and the surrounding hills. This is a popular spot for watching the sunset, as the sun dips below the horizon and paints the sky with vibrant colors.

4. Tongariro Alpine Crossing

The red cinder, the sulphur yellow, the steam from vents — the alien quality of it is amplified by low sunlight. Everything goes theatrical.

5. Lake Tekapo, Mackenzie

The Church of the Good Shepherd silhouetted against a sky that starts pale yellow and ends purple through every shade of pink and orange. The lake is turquoise. The Mackenzie Basin is so far from light pollution that stars become visible while there's still colour in the west. I sat on the grass above the church for two hours and missed dinner and did not mind at all.
