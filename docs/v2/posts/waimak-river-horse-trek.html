I've always wanted to try horse riding, and when I saw that there was a riding centre in Christchurch that offered beginner friendly treks, I knew I had to give it a go. We signed up with Waimak River Riding Centre through BookMe for a scenic 1 hour trek along the Waimakariri River. Unfortunately, they seem to have permanently closed.

Upon arrival, we were greeted by an overly enthusiatic dog who came up to us and sniffed my um, private areas, which was how I found out that this is actually a pretty normal thing for dogs to do??? We were then sat down for a quick briefing and to get fitted for our helmets, before being led to a round pen to meet our horses.

In the pen, the staff gave a demonstation on how to mount and dismount the horses, as well as how to hold the reins, speed up, slow down, stop and turn. To get the horses moving, you have to kind of squeeze their sides with your legs and dig in your heels a little. You can also click your tongue at them, but that didn't come naturally to me 😅 To slow down or stop, pull on both sides of the reins equally, and to turn, pull on one side of the reins. When going up a slope, lean forward in your saddle, and when going downhill, lean back.

After covering all the basics, we were then each assigned a horse according to our weight and build. My horse (mare) was named Diva, and all I can say is that she lived up to her name.

Phones weren't allowed on the trek for safety reasons, although our lead guide, Alannah, assured us that she'll help to take some photos. I did feel kind of bad about it as I was the first in line after her, and in the photos she took, you couldn't really see anyone else clearly apart from me and the girl after me 😅 Lucky me, I suppose!

It was also during the trek that I learned that horses have a very strict herd pecking order, which would determine who goes where in the single-file lineup required in an organised trail ride like this. Diva was originally supposed to be second in line, but apparently she really likes being first, and despite my best efforts to pull her back, she ended up surging forward and overtaking the first horse (Princess) pretty early on in the trek.

I'm not sure if my experience was an anomaly or the norm, but we were told the horses were more difficult to manage than usual that day. As some of the horses stopped to graze on the grass (we were encouraged to pull them along and not let them stop), or to poop (which they do a lot), our single-file formation eventually became messier and messier, with horses straying off to the side or attempting to overtake each other.

At several points, we had to stop and reorganise. One of the guides stationed in the middle of the formation got off her horse to help, and struggled getting on again. During the wait, some of the horses started getting more and more agitated, especially Alannah's, whom she mentioned did not like stopping for long. We had to continue our journey, leaving the other 2 guides on the trek with us to manage as best as they could with the second half of the group.

At some point, Princess got too close to Diva's heels and was greeted with a kick, which sent her to her knees. Luckily, her rider wasn't thrown off. We were only halfway through our trek at this juncture, and I was starting to feel a little nervous about the rest of it.

The next challenge was the river crossing, which Alannah warned is usually the most difficult segment. We were instructed to stick to our formation tightly and to complete the crossing asap... which I discovered was easier said than done. Right off the bat as we crossed, the horses started to stop haphazardly to drink from the river, and despite trying to nudge them along, they exercised what limited free will they have to the fullest extent.

Needless to say, by the time we were all across the river, the formation was completely jumbled. While the guides were trying to get the horses back in line, a farmer nearby started coming closer with a tractor, which seemingly spooked the horses. This was when Alannah yelled out that these horses are retired race horses, and that if they are spooked and take off, then we are doomed LOL. So we all had to sit tight and hope for the best, and just start setting off as is.

Thankfully, the journey back was less eventful, and we all made it back to the pen in one piece. After dismounting, we handed back our helmets, and I headed back to the pen to snap some photos with Diva. Unfortunately, the lighting wasn't very good with the sun beating down on us, and in any case, Diva was too busy munching on grass to pay any attention to me.

All in all, while it was a little chaotic at times, I still enjoyed the experience and would highly recommend everyone to try it out. Always remember that while the horses are trained for treks like this, they are still highly complex sentient beings with their own personalities and quirks, who can feel and feed off your energy. So just relax, trust your horse, and enjoy the ride!
