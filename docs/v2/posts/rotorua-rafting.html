Rafting down the Kaituna River is one of the most thrilling adventures you can experience in New Zealand. The river offers a perfect mix of gentle rapids and exciting challenges, making it suitable for both beginners and experienced rafters.

Activity Summary

Operator: Rotorua Rafting
Price: $63 NZD
Duration: ~2 hours

There are a few tour operators offering similar experiences for rafting down the Kaituna River, all with glowing reviews. We managed to snag a pretty good deal for Rotorua Rafting on BookMe for just $63 NZD per person, but you won't go wrong with any of them!

All necessary equipment was provided including wetsuits, booties, helmets and life jackets, so all you have to do is show up 15 min early and get fitted. There are lockers provided to store your belongings. Once everyone was ready, we boarded the van that would take us to the river which was a short distance away.

We shared the raft with a lovely mother and son duo who are Chinese Australian, under the hands of a chill guide who kept us very safe, which I really appreciated! Some of the other rafts had more raucous crowds who seemed to push for more of a thrill factor and their guides were happy to oblige (spoiler alert: everyone there went in the water a few times), so pick your crowd carefully if you are an anxious participant like me.

Before we set off, we were taught the brace position, which is crucial for the big drop at Tutea Falls. Basically, hold on tight to the paddle and ropes at the side, tuck in and keep our heads down. We practiced the motions, recited a Maori chant together (for safety and blessing) and then it was action time!

During the course, we were guided through 2 smaller drops to get us accustomed to what would come, which really helped to ease my nerves. Surprisingly, I found myself enjoying it and having fun, a far cry from my initial anxiety about the whole thing.

This was the second "drop" and we went in sideways on this one!

The highlight of the course, Tutea Falls, came sooner than I felt ready for. The guide will stop the raft shortly before the drop to give us a final briefing on what to expect and what to do if the raft tips over, since there is a very real chance of it happening. Out of the 5 or 6 rafts that set off that day, 2 of them ended up tipping over.

We were instructed to reposition ourselves one row back as we didn't have a full raft. Once we each gave the thumbs up, our guide slowly navigated us towards the Falls, and before I knew it, we were at the edge, and then... we were doing down.
